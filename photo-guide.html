Adding a finished job

About two minutes per car, on your phone. You never touch the website, and nothing you submit goes live on its own.

Open the job upload form

Save the form to your phone

On iPhone, open the form in Safari, tap the share button, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the three dots, then Add to Home screen. After that it's one tap.

You have to be signed in to a Google account to attach photos. That's Google's rule for file uploads, not ours.

The one question that matters

Tell us about this job. Two or three sentences, like you'd tell the customer standing next to the car.

Came in with the rear bumper cover cracked and the quarter panel creased. Pulled the panel back rather than replacing it. Silver is a tricky match so we blended into the door.

That's a good answer.

Why it matters this much. The website writes the page for you, but it can only describe what it can see in the photos. It doesn't know the car came in on a tow truck, or that the color was discontinued, or that you saved the customer a whole panel. Those details are what make the page worth reading, and Google ranks pages people actually read.

Stick to the car. Don't put customer names, plate numbers, addresses, claim numbers, or anything about the customer's own business in this box.

Taking the photos

Shoot the before and the after from the same spot. Same angle, same distance, same side of the car. This matters more than camera quality, because the website builds a slider people drag across to compare the two, and it only lines up when the photos match.

Keep the license plate out of the shot when you can. If it ends up in frame, it gets blurred automatically before the page goes live, and so does anything else personal, like faces or paperwork.

iPhone owners, change one setting once. Go to Settings, Camera, Formats, and choose Most Compatible. By default an iPhone saves photos in a format the website can't read, and those get skipped. You only have to do this once.

What happens next

Within six hours the website picks up your submission and prepares a draft page with your photos and a written description.

The draft doesn't go live. License plates, faces and other personal details are blurred automatically. Someone then reviews it, fixes anything the computer got wrong, and publishes it. Once published, the car shows on the Our Work page, and the four most recent jobs show on the front page.

Quick checklist

  • Before and after shot from the same angle
  • License plate out of the shot where possible
  • iPhone set to Most Compatible
  • Two or three real sentences in "Tell us about this job"
  • No customer names, plates, claim numbers or business details
  • Year left blank rather than guessed
