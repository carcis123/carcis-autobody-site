# Putting a finished job on the website

For Carlos and Kayla. It takes about two minutes per car, on your phone.

There is one form. Fill it in, attach the photos, done. You never touch the
website, and nothing you submit goes live on its own.

**The form:** https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSdcndAhsM_1NKZmU5_mmqHHQLn0Vmv8JSeDUpssMqA7HkU-wQ/viewform

Save it to your phone's home screen. On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap the
share button, then **Add to Home Screen**. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap
the three dots, then **Add to Home screen**.

---

## What the form asks

| Question | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Date the job was finished | Not today's date, the day the car was done |
| Vehicle year | Leave blank if you are not sure. Do not guess |
| Vehicle make and model | Toyota, RAV4. Close is fine, typos get fixed |
| Color | Maroon, white, black |
| What work did you do? | Pick the closest one |
| Was this an insurance claim? | Yes or no |
| Tell us about this job | The important one, see below |
| Before photos | Up to 5 |
| After photos | Up to 5 |

You have to be signed in to a Google account to attach photos. That is Google's
rule for file uploads, not ours.

---

## The one question that matters

**Tell us about this job.** Two or three sentences, like you would tell the
customer standing next to the car.

> Came in with the rear bumper cover cracked and the quarter panel creased.
> Pulled the panel back rather than replacing it. Silver is a tricky match so
> we blended into the door.

That is it. That is a good answer.

**Why it matters this much.** The website writes the page for you, but it can
only describe what it can see in the photos. It does not know the car came in
on a tow truck, or that the color was discontinued, or that you saved the
customer a whole panel. Those details are what make the page worth reading,
and Google ranks pages that people actually read. A page with nothing real in
it can do more harm than good.

Do not put customer names, plate numbers, addresses or claim numbers in this
box. The page is public.

---

## Taking the photos

**Shoot the before and the after from the same spot.** Same angle, same
distance, same side of the car. This matters more than camera quality, because
the website builds a slider that people drag across to compare the two, and it
only works properly when the two photos line up.

**Keep the license plate out of the shot when you can.** If it ends up in
frame, that's fine. It gets blurred automatically before the page goes live,
and so does anything else personal, like faces or paperwork.

**iPhone owners, change one setting once.** Go to **Settings**, **Camera**,
**Formats**, and choose **Most Compatible**.

By default an iPhone saves photos in a format the website cannot read, and
those get skipped. This makes it save normal JPEGs instead. You only have to do
it once, and it applies to every photo you take from then on.

---

## What happens next

Within six hours the website picks up your submission and prepares a draft
page with your photos and a written description.

**The draft does not go live.** License plates, faces and other personal
details are blurred automatically. Someone then reviews it, fixes anything the
computer got wrong, and publishes it. Anything the computer was unsure about is
flagged for that person to check.

Once published, the car appears on the **Our Work** page, and the four most
recent jobs show on the front page.

---

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Before and after shot from the same angle
- [ ] iPhone set to Most Compatible
- [ ] Two or three real sentences in "Tell us about this job"
- [ ] No customer names, plates or claim numbers
- [ ] Year left blank rather than guessed
