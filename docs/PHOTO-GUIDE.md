# Putting a finished job on the website

For Carlos and Kayla. This takes about two minutes per car.

Everything happens in the shared Google Drive folder. You do not touch the
website. Nothing you upload goes live on its own.

---

## One folder per car

Make a new folder inside the shared folder. Name it like this:

```
2026-09-12 Toyota Corolla - rear bumper
```

Three parts, in order:

1. **The date the job finished**, written `YYYY-MM-DD`. If you leave it off,
   the website uses the day you made the folder.
2. **The vehicle.** Make and model. Add the year only if you actually know it.
3. **What you did**, after a dash. A few words is plenty.

---

## Naming the photos

Put the word **before** or **after** in the filename. That is the only rule.

```
before-1.jpg
before-2.jpg
after-1.jpg
after-2.jpg
```

`Before 1.jpg`, `corolla_before.jpg` and `BEFORE-3.jpg` all work too.

When there is both a before and an after, the website builds the slider that
people drag across to compare the two. Photos without either word still get
published, they just do not go in the slider.

Up to six photos per car make it onto the page. Put the best ones first.

### Shoot the before and the after from the same spot

This matters more than camera quality. Same angle, same distance, same side of
the car. The slider only works properly when the two photos line up.

### iPhone owners, change one setting once

**Settings**, **Camera**, **Formats**, choose **Most Compatible**.

By default an iPhone saves photos in a format the website cannot read, and
those photos get skipped. This setting makes it save normal JPEGs instead. You
only have to do it once.

---

## Say something about the job

Add a file called `notes.txt` in the folder with two or three sentences. This
is the most valuable thing you can do and it takes a minute.

Write what you would tell a customer standing next to the car:

> Came in with the rear bumper cover cracked and the quarter panel creased.
> Pulled the panel back rather than replacing it. Silver is a tricky match so
> we blended into the door.

You can write it on your phone. Google Drive, plus button, new text file.

**Why it matters.** The website writes a draft description from your photos on
its own, but it can only describe what it sees. It does not know the car came
in on a tow truck, or that the colour was discontinued, or that you saved the
customer a panel replacement. Those details are what make the page worth
reading, and Google ranks pages people actually read. A page with nothing real
in it can do more harm than good.

Do not put customer names, plate numbers, addresses or claim numbers in the
notes. The page is public.

---

## What happens next

Within six hours the website picks up the folder and prepares a draft page with
your photos and a written description.

**The draft does not go live.** Someone reviews it first, fixes anything the
computer got wrong, and publishes it. Anything the computer was unsure about is
flagged for that person to check.

Once it is published the car appears on the **Our Work** page, and the four
most recent jobs show on the front page.

---

## Quick checklist

- [ ] New folder, named `date vehicle - what you did`
- [ ] Photos have **before** or **after** in the filename
- [ ] Before and after shot from the same angle
- [ ] iPhone set to Most Compatible
- [ ] `notes.txt` with two or three real sentences
- [ ] No customer names, plates or claim numbers
